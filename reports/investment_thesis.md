# Salik — Financial Analysis & Valuation Framework

## Business overview
Salik operates Dubai's barrier-free road tolling network under a long-term concession with Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority. Its core economics are driven by chargeable traffic, tariff structure, the toll network footprint and concession economics. Ancillary services may provide incremental diversification.

## Performance thesis
FY2025 was a step-change year: reported revenue increased 35.1%, EBITDA increased 35.8%, and net profit increased 33.4% versus FY2024. Management attributed the increase largely to variable pricing introduced in January 2025 and the first full year of operation of two new gates that opened in November 2024.

## What could sustain growth?
- Growth in Dubai's resident, tourist and business populations may support traffic demand.
- Variable pricing can alter peak/off-peak revenue yield and traffic distribution.
- New services such as parking payment solutions may expand the addressable opportunity.
- A highly automated operating model supports strong margins.

## Key risks
- Regulatory and concession concentration: operations depend heavily on the RTA concession and its terms.
- Traffic and economic sensitivity: weaker traffic or changes in commuting patterns could pressure revenue.
- Pricing sensitivity: tariff changes may affect both trip volumes and timing.
- Leverage and finance costs: debt servicing influences equity cash flow and dividends.
- Fine collection and impairment: fines are a meaningful revenue category and receivables can carry collection risk.
- Terminal value: the finite concession horizon should be considered in any long-term valuation rather than blindly assuming a perpetual business.

## Valuation framework
The repository includes an illustrative EV/EBITDA sensitivity using analyst-selected multiples. These are scenario inputs, not observed market multiples. The peer template is intentionally blank where current market data is required. A robust comps analysis needs a shared valuation date, market capitalization, net debt, LTM EBITDA, concession duration, growth profile and accounting consistency.

## Conclusion
The central question is how much FY2025's step-change reflects repeatable traffic/pricing dynamics versus a non-recurring comparison effect. A scenario-based forecast should separate trip growth, average revenue per trip, tariff mix and ancillary revenues before using a valuation multiple.
